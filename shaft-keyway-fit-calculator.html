    What to verify before specifying a part

    Measure the actual components

    Record shaft and bore diameters, slot width/depth, key length, and any wear or damage. Confirm measuring tools and datum locations.

    Confirm fits and load cases

    Use the correct tolerance system and account for torque reversals, shock, starts, stops, temperature, and the weakest loaded component.

    Have the design reviewed

    Key and keyseat standards define dimensions and tolerances; strength analysis requires a separate design check for the assembly.

    How the preliminary outputs are calculated

    Metric torque estimate: T = 9,550 × power (kW) ÷ speed (rpm) × selected service factor. Nominal tangential force: F = 2T ÷ shaft diameter, with consistent units. These simplified relationships do not establish the actual load path or approve a shaft, key, hub, sleeve, or adapter.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does a larger bore automatically mean a sleeve will work?

    No. A diameter difference alone does not establish a safe sleeve or adapter. The design also depends on fits, concentricity, wall thickness, material, load, geometry, retention, and the mating components.

    Can this tool select a key or approve a stepped key?

    No. It records slot measurements and shows their differences. It does not establish key dimensions, engagement, strength, or suitability when the slots differ.

    Can I send the printout to a machine shop?

    Yes, as a preliminary measurement brief. Mark it as unverified and ask the shop or engineer to confirm the dimensions and design before quoting or manufacturing.

    Does the force result represent the actual load on the key?

    No. It is a nominal tangential force estimate derived from entered power, speed, service factor, and shaft diameter. Transient torque, gear ratio, load sharing, geometry, and operating conditions can change actual loads.

    Standards and calculation traceability

    Metric geometry reference: DIN 6885-1:2021-11 is listed as current by DIN Media. Imperial geometry reference: ASME B17.1-1967 (S2023) covers key/keyseat sizes and tolerances, but explicitly excludes key strength analysis. The dimension tables are not embedded in this preview, so it does not auto-select a standard key. ISO/R 773:1969 is withdrawn; it may appear in legacy drawings but is not presented here as current.

    Stress methods: conventional machine-design shear and bearing equations require a defined load path, effective key dimensions, and material allowables. Those strength calculations and material values are not active in this preview; they must be validated against a licensed reference and engineering review before release. Fit classes are also not auto-recommended. For fit nomenclature, ISO 286 is used in metric contexts; ANSI B4.1 is the inch-system catalog reference. ANSI B4.2 is for preferred metric limits and fits.
