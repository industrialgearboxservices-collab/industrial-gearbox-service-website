Motovario Gearbox Repair & Model Identification

Direct answer: Motovario offers several reducer families with different gear arrangements and mounting options. Record whether the unit is an H, B, S, NMRV or other series, then match its size and configuration to the correct product documentation.

Start with the series name and complete type code

The product code changes the identification path: H denotes a helical range, B a helical-bevel family, S a shaft-mounted line, and NMRV/NMRV POWER worm reducers have their own combinations and execution options. The same manufacturer name therefore does not tell a technician which manual, mounting drawing, or parts list applies.

Motovario model families at a glance

Motovario’s published industrial range includes H helical, B helical-bevel, S shaft-mounted, and NMRV/NMRV POWER worm reducers.

Inspection questions specific to this configuration

A useful Motovario inquiry starts with the series prefix and size. Add the full combination code when present, any pre-stage or combined reducer, flange and shaft dimensions, and the motor frame/interface. For worm units, capture the orientation and mounting position; for S-series units, photograph the hollow-shaft and torque-arm arrangement.

Questions about Motovario equipment

How can I tell which Motovario series I have?

Read the full type plate and look for the series designation, size, ratio, mounting position, and any combination or pre-stage code.

Are Motovario H and B gearboxes the same design?

No. Motovario describes H as helical and B as helical-bevel. Use the series-specific manual and drawing for identification.

What information matters for an NMRV reducer?

Record its NMRV or NMRV POWER designation, size, ratio, mounting position, flange, shaft dimensions, and whether it is combined with another reducer.

Can I replace a Motovario reducer by matching its frame size?

Frame size alone is insufficient. Check ratio, stages, shaft layout, motor adapter, mounting, load requirements, and accessories.

Should I send a photo of the motor too?

Yes. A geared assembly may have separate motor and reducer identification. Photograph both plates and show how the motor joins the reducer.
