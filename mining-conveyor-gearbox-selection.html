Mining Conveyor Gearboxes: What to Verify Before Replacement

A right-angle gearbox proposal is only useful when its ratings, interfaces, sealing, cooling, and maintenance requirements match the conveyor and the site. This guide gives maintenance and purchasing teams a concise way to compare the written specifications before approving a replacement.

Editorial note: The source brief supplied for this page did not identify a specific new product or manufacturer release. This is an equipment-selection guide, not a report of a recent launch.

Mining conveyors, bucket elevators, and reclaiming systems can impose different loads and installation constraints. Product descriptions may use similar terms—right-angle, bevel-helical, heavy-duty—but those labels alone do not establish suitability. Compare the actual unit data and the conveyor duty with the manufacturer’s selection documentation.

Start with the conveyor duty and load profile

Record the driven machine, required output speed, operating hours, starts and stops, reversing requirements, incline, and known transient or impact loads. Confirm the motor input, ratio, required output torque, and any service or application factors against the drive supplier’s selection method. Do not substitute a general “heavy-duty” description for a reviewed duty calculation.

For a replacement, compare the original gearbox nameplate and drawings with the proposed unit. A ratio or power rating that appears close may still differ in output speed, shaft direction, load capacity, or thermal suitability.

Check mounting, shaft, and installation interfaces

Confirm mounting position, foot or flange pattern, shaft height, input and output arrangement, hollow-bore or solid-shaft details, key or shrink-disc interface, and available service clearance. Verify whether the proposal changes coupling, base, backstop, or guarding requirements. Record each difference for the mechanical and operations teams to review before the outage.

Ask for the dimensional drawing and installation instructions for the exact proposed model and configuration. A product-family brochure may not include every option or interface needed for the specific conveyor.

Review sealing, lubrication, and cooling as configured options

Dust protection, lubricant retention, and heat rejection depend on the selected sealing and lubrication arrangements, operating conditions, and installation. Taconite seals, labyrinth seals, fans, and forced lubrication are configuration-specific features; none should be assumed from a general product label. Verify the exact bill of materials, lubricant requirements, ambient conditions, speed, and thermal limits in the supplier’s documentation.

Published manufacturer examples illustrate why the exact model matters. TANHON’s mining-gearbox page describes several distinct conveyor and crusher configurations, including bevel-helical units and selected sealing or cooling features. Those descriptions apply to the listed products, not automatically to other gearboxes or this site’s service capability. Source: TANHON mining gearbox solutions.

Make maintainability part of the comparison

Compare access to inspection points, oil drains and breathers, seal locations, cooling components, and lifting or removal points. Ask what routine checks the supplier recommends and which parts are replaceable in the installed arrangement. Include spare-parts identification, lead times, and the maintenance documents supplied with the unit in the proposal review.

Use a short replacement review before approval

  1. Capture the existing nameplate, model, ratio, mounting position, and shaft interfaces.
  2. Document conveyor duty, operating pattern, motor data, and relevant load events.
  3. Compare the proposed ratings and dimensions with the exact application and installation drawings.
  4. Confirm sealing, lubrication, cooling, environmental limits, and maintenance requirements for the quoted configuration.
  5. Identify changes to the base, coupling, backstop, controls, guarding, lifting, commissioning, and spares.
  6. Keep unresolved assumptions visible and obtain engineering approval against the site’s own requirements.

This checklist helps organize a comparison; it does not select, certify, or approve a gearbox. Final selection depends on the equipment data, manufacturer guidance, and qualified engineering review for the application.
