Gearbox Nameplate Field Guide & Calculators

Use this guide to read common gearbox and motor plate fields, estimate basic drive relationships, and gather the details a repair evaluator needs. Labels and rating conventions vary by manufacturer and model.

Gearbox calculators

Enter positive numeric values from the equipment record. For an installed drive, actual operating shaft speed is more useful than motor synchronous speed.

Output speed and torque

Enter values to calculate.

Uses output power = input power × efficiency, output rpm = input rpm ÷ ratio, and torque (N·m) = 9549.3 × output kW ÷ output rpm. Efficiency is an estimate you supply.

Ratio from measured speeds

Enter values to calculate.

Ratio = input speed ÷ output speed. Measure under representative operation and confirm the manufacturer’s rated ratio before ordering parts.

Motor synchronous speed

Synchronous speed: 1,800 rpm

Formula: 120 × frequency ÷ poles. An induction motor’s full-load speed is typically lower because of slip; use the actual motor plate or measured speed for gearbox calculations.

Service-factor margin check

Enter values to calculate.

Compares the entered application torque × required factor with the entered unit rating. Confirm how the manufacturer defines rated torque and service factor; avoid applying a factor twice.

Former AGMA number / ISO VG viscosity reference

ISO VG 220 corresponds to former AGMA grade 5 by viscosity.

This is a viscosity cross-reference only. It does not establish oil chemistry, additive package, approval, or suitability. Modern AGMA standards use ISO viscosity grades and retain former AGMA numbers as a reference; follow the exact gearbox manual and do not change or mix oils based on this converter.

Nameplate field dictionary

Plate labels can vary by brand, age, language, and region. Search the field names below. Copy the full type code exactly and photograph the plate before interpreting it.

Type or model designation

Also shown as Type, Typ, Model, Catalog No., or Part No. Identifies the product family and configuration; code formats vary. Copy every letter, number, dash, and suffix.

Serial number

Also shown as S/N, Serial, Ser. No., or Fabr.-Nr. Identifies the individual unit and may help locate build or parts records when the manufacturer retains them.

Order, job, or sales number

May connect the unit to its original order and configuration records. Preserve it with the type and serial number.

Ratio (i)

Reduction ratio is commonly expressed as input speed divided by output speed. Exact ratios may include decimals; do not round a plate value for parts identification.

Input speed (n1)

Rated rotational speed at the gearbox input shaft, usually expressed in rpm. Check the operating configuration and drive speed used for the rating.

Output speed (n2)

Rated rotational speed at the gearbox output shaft, usually expressed in rpm for the specified input speed and ratio.

Power rating (P, kW, HP)

Rated power associated with the unit and its stated conditions. Confirm units and whether a figure is mechanical, thermal, or limited by another condition.

Output torque (T2, M2)

Rated output torque, often N·m or lb·in. Torque, power, and speed are related; the plate’s exact rating and conditions take precedence over estimates.

Service factor (SF, fB)

A design/application factor associated with duty and loading. A gearbox rating and an application-required factor are different values; check how the specific manufacturer defines each.

Mechanical and thermal ratings

Some units list mechanical rating and thermal capacity separately. Thermal limits depend on operating conditions, ambient temperature, mounting, cooling, and lubrication; use the exact manual.

Mounting position or arrangement

Gearbox orientation codes can determine oil level, fill/drain locations, and vent position. Codes are manufacturer-specific; do not treat a motor IM code as the gearbox’s mounting code.

Lubricant type and viscosity

May show oil family, viscosity grade, or manufacturer approval. ISO VG describes viscosity at 40 °C; it does not by itself identify chemistry or approvals. Confirm the exact lubricant in the unit manual.

Oil quantity or fill

A listed quantity can be associated with a particular mounting position. Set oil level using the specified level device and procedure, not volume alone.

Rotation arrow, backstop, or holdback

May identify a permitted direction or a non-reverse device. Confirm configuration and rotation before startup, especially after motor or wiring work.

Efficiency (η)

Efficiency is the fraction of input power delivered as output power at stated conditions. Actual efficiency varies with design, stage count, load, speed, and temperature.

Weight or mass

Check whether the listed mass is dry or includes oil/accessories. Use the actual unit’s lifting and shipping instructions.

Ambient temperature

May specify the environmental condition used for a thermal rating or allowed operation. Higher ambient temperature can affect allowable continuous load.

Date of manufacture

May be printed directly or encoded in a serial number. Keep original documents if the date is not shown.

Shaft diameter, bore, and keyway

These dimensions are often absent from nameplates. Measure the actual parts with suitable tools; see the shaft and keyway fit calculator for measurement planning.

Backlash

Free movement between gear elements, sometimes stated in arcminutes. Its importance depends on the gearbox design and application; compare only with the correct model specification.

Motor voltage, frequency, and current

These are motor electrical data, not gearbox rating fields. Photograph the motor plate separately from the gearbox plate.

Motor frame, insulation class, and duty

Frame size describes motor mounting dimensions. Insulation class and duty code describe motor design/operating conditions; verify motor data from its own plate and documentation.

Ingress protection (IP)

An enclosure rating commonly shown on the motor plate. Confirm which component the IP code applies to; it does not describe gearbox internal condition.

Sources & calculation method

  • NORD B1000 operating and installation manual — example of manufacturer-specific plate fields, lubrication, mounting, and operating guidance.
  • IEC 60034-7:2020 — official IM code standard for rotating electrical machines.
  • WEG motor mounting reference — illustrative motor arrangement codes; consult the motor’s own documentation.
  • ANSI/AGMA 9005-F16 — industrial gear lubrication standard; the former AGMA number is a viscosity reference, not a complete lubricant approval.
  • Petro-Canada viscosity cross-reference — ISO VG and former AGMA viscosity-grade correspondence table.

Calculators use standard relationships: output speed = input speed ÷ ratio; ratio = input speed ÷ output speed; torque (N·m) = 9549.3 × power (kW) ÷ speed (rpm); synchronous speed (rpm) = 120 × frequency (Hz) ÷ pole count. Horsepower conversion uses 1 HP = 0.7457 kW; N·m to lb·in uses 1 N·m = 8.8507 lb·in. Results are estimates from entered values and are not manufacturer ratings.

Lubricant grade reference maps ISO VG viscosity grades to former AGMA viscosity numbers as listed in the cited cross-reference. It does not identify lubricant chemistry, additives, compounded/synthetic type, or manufacturer approval. The gearbox maker’s instructions govern.

Frequently asked questions

Is the motor nameplate the same as the gearbox nameplate?

No. Geared motors can have separate plates. Record and photograph each plate and identify which component it belongs to.

Can I use these calculations to select a replacement gearbox?

No. They estimate basic relationships from entered data. Selection requires manufacturer ratings and the actual application duty, mounting, service factor, thermal conditions, interfaces, and other model-specific requirements.

Can I change oil based on the ISO VG to AGMA reference?

No. The table compares viscosity grades only. Lubricant chemistry, additives, approvals, temperature, and gearbox design matter. Follow the gearbox manufacturer’s oil specification.

What if the gearbox plate is missing or unreadable?

Photograph the whole gearbox, shaft ends, feet, motor, and connected machine. Include dimensions, operating speed, application, old drawings, and any remaining stamped numbers in the repair brief.
