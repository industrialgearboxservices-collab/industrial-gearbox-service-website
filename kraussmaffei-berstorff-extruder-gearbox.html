KraussMaffei Berstorff Extruder Gearbox Identification & Repair Planning

For KraussMaffei and legacy Berstorff extruders, identify the machine and drive as a complete assembly. Extruder family, screw arrangement, gearbox designation, and motor details help define the right inspection question.

KraussMaffei consolidated the KraussMaffei Berstorff name within its unified brand, while older equipment and documents may still use Berstorff. Published material describes gear-unit configurations in specific extrusion systems, including twin-screw examples. Those machine examples do not establish that every model uses the same gearbox.

Use model examples as identification clues, not interchange rules

Manufacturer publications describe the ZE BluePower co-rotating twin-screw series and a KMD 35-26/L counter-rotating laboratory extruder with a high-torque gearbox. These are different machine examples. Do not assume gearbox, screw, or parts interchangeability from a shared manufacturer name or extrusion application.

Gearbox issue or process issue?

Changes in output, torque demand, or process stability can have multiple causes. Record gearbox observations separately from material, temperature, screw speed, and control changes. A process symptom alone does not prove gearbox damage.

Prepare an extruder-drive inquiry

Include machine and gearbox plates, full-unit and coupling photos, screw configuration, process and load history, current symptoms, oil details, drawings, and records of prior modifications. KraussMaffei and Berstorff questions

Are KraussMaffei and Berstorff both useful names when identifying older equipment?

Yes. Older machine plates and documents may use KraussMaffei Berstorff, while current materials use the unified KraussMaffei brand. Record the name exactly as it appears on the equipment.

Does the extruder model identify the gearbox?

No. Record the machine model and serial number plus any separate gearbox and motor plate details. Do not infer the gearbox configuration from the extruder name alone.

Can an output or process change confirm gearbox failure?

No. Material, process settings, motor, coupling, screws, and gearbox can all affect operation. Record process changes and mechanical observations separately, then use inspection to determine the cause.
