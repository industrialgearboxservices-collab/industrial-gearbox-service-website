Pump symptoms and what to measure

SymptomPossible areasUseful evidence
Low flow or pressureSystem resistance, suction, speed, blockage, air, internal wearFlow, pressure tap locations, speed, pump curve, fluid
Rough or gravel-like operationCavitation, air, recirculation, bearings, installationSuction, fluid temperature, operating point, sound/vibration location
Seal leakageSeal faces, shaft movement, installation, materials, auxiliary systemLeak point, seal type, fluid, operating history
Repeated bearing damageFits, lubrication, alignment, piping loads, contamination, dutyFailure pattern, installation history, previous reports

KSB's cavitation reference explains the mechanism. Sound alone is not conclusive. Centrifugal and positive-displacement pumps have different behavior and protection requirements; follow the actual equipment instructions.

Disassembly and hydraulic inspection

Record orientation, part identity, deposits, rubbing, erosion, corrosion, and damage before cleaning. Measure specified clearances where design and scope call for them. Inspect impeller, passages, casing, and wear components against applicable dimensions. Distinguish removable deposits from material loss.

Connect each finding to pump function and duty. Erosion, corrosion, blockage, and wear affect performance differently. A cleaned component is not automatically acceptable; document measurements and disposition.

Shaft, bearings, seals, and repair scope

Inspect shaft geometry, journals, sleeves, seal interfaces, bearing supports, and fits as needed. Review the seal type and relevant flush, barrier, or cooling provisions. Replacing a seal should account for conditions that damaged it when supported by findings.

Define components to restore or replace, dimensions, clearances, procedures, exclusions, records, and approval for scope changes before authorizing work.

Assembly and acceptance checks

Reassemble to applicable design requirements and procedures. Pressure or performance testing, if required and available, needs defined conditions and acceptance criteria. State what was tested and what could not be reproduced, such as process fluid, temperature, or installed duty.

After installation, follow the plant's approved procedure for alignment, piping/base condition, seal preparation, priming where required, guarding, and operating checks. Separate workshop acceptance from site commissioning.

When should a pump be repaired or replaced?

Repair may fit when inspection supports a defined restoration and the hydraulic design remains suitable for the required duty. Review replacement when damage is extensive, requirements cannot be met, or process duty has changed. Compare freight, installation, alignment, piping or control changes, schedule, and commissioning.

For a connected drive issue, also review the motor repair guide and system overview. Confirm the actual provider, facilities, qualifications, and scope for the specific pump before shipment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I diagnose a pump that will not reach flow?

Record speed, fluid, suction and discharge conditions, system configuration, and instrument locations. Compare the measured operating point with the pump curve and system duty before attributing the problem to internal wear.

Can cavitation be fixed by replacing pump bearings?

No. Bearings do not correct the inlet or operating conditions that can cause cavitation. Investigate suction, fluid temperature, operating point, and resulting damage.

Why can a new mechanical seal leak?

Possible contributors include installation, shaft movement, materials, operating conditions, and auxiliary seal systems. Review the complete sealing arrangement and service conditions.

What is checked during a pump overhaul?

A scope may record disassembly condition, measure specified clearances, inspect hydraulic components, shaft, bearings, and seals, then reassemble and perform agreed checks. Exact work depends on design and findings.

Does a low discharge-pressure reading prove impeller wear?

No. Speed, suction, system resistance, fluid properties, and instrument condition can also affect pressure. Compare full operating conditions with the relevant pump information.

What information should I send about a pump?
