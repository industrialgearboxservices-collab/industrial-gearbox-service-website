Squeegee Wringer Roller Repair

Squeegee or wringer rolls remove or squeeze liquid from a moving web or strip as it passes through a nip. The process liquid and substrate affect material compatibility and the required contact conditions.

Prepare a repair brief ↗

What should I document before requesting roller repair?

  • Identify the process liquid, material, and line section; provide applicable safety and chemical records.
  • Record the paired roll arrangement, face dimensions, covering specification, and settings from plant documentation.
  • Describe residual liquid, streaks, edge effects, corrosion, or cover marks across the material.

Before shipment, confirm the agreed service scope, schedule, and receiving arrangements.

Technical context

EPA process guidance on wringer rolls describes wringer rolls in a specific wet-process context; material and nip requirements differ by application.

What liquid and roll pair are involved in a squeegee or wringer issue?

Squeegee or wringer rolls can remove excess liquid as strip or web passes through a paired-roll nip. In a documented blank-and-strip washer, the roll pair squeezes oil, emulsion, or water from metal parts or strip; nip pressure affects residual liquid. Other wet-process lines may use different materials and designs.

Identify the liquid and substrate, line section, paired-roll arrangement, cover material, and recorded nip settings. Describe streaks or uneven carryover by location across the material. Avoid prescribing an adjustment without the machine maker’s specifications. See the JVM strip washer reference; use the plant’s required guarding and safety procedures around nip rolls.

Squeegee Wringer Roller Repair FAQs

What liquid information matters for wringer roller repair?

Identify the process solution from plant records, the material being treated, and the line conditions. Chemical compatibility and safety details should be available before discussing a covering or surface requirement.

Can uneven liquid removal be diagnosed from a wringer roll alone?

No. The paired roll, nip setup, material, solution, and line conditions can all matter. Document the pattern and existing settings rather than assuming the roll is the sole cause.
